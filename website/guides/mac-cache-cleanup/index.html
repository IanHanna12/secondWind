Caches

Which Mac caches are safe to remove?

“Cache” describes a purpose, not a universal safety guarantee. Its path, owner, and the app that uses it still matter.

Why broad cache deletion is risky

Some cached data is easily recreated. Other folders beside it can contain application state, user-created work, shared resources, or data whose ownership is ambiguous. A script that removes every folder named “cache” cannot make that distinction reliably.

What Second Wind considers

Cleanup eligibility comes from a known location and a rule with an approved route. The app can show an item without making it actionable. Protected, ambiguous, Apple-owned, and user-data-bearing locations remain outside the cleanup plan.

How to review an eligible cache

  1. Read the item’s explanation and rule.
  2. Confirm the displayed path belongs to the app or tool you expect.
  3. Choose only the entries you want in the plan.
  4. Use the final review to choose Finder Trash or local Recovery.
  5. Afterward, check the recorded result rather than assuming all planned bytes became free space.

Orphaned app data needs more caution

A folder with an uninstalled app’s identifier is not automatically safe. Second Wind limits reviewed orphan cleanup to exact third-party cache and log paths. Apple-owned identifiers, Application Support, Containers, Preferences, saved state, and ambiguous matches stay protected.

Common questions

Will an application recreate its cache?

A known cache is generally intended to be recreated, but rebuilding may require time, network access, or a fresh application launch. That does not make every neighbouring folder safe. Review the matched rule, exact location, ownership explanation, and supported destination for the individual entry.

Why is a visible cache protected?

Visibility and cleanup eligibility are separate. A path may be useful for explaining storage while its ownership, contents, approved root, or recovery behavior remains uncertain. In that case Second Wind keeps it visible and non-actionable rather than converting limited evidence into permission to move data.

Rule of thumb

Remove a cache only when you can identify the exact location, why it was observed, and what will happen if the owning app recreates it.
